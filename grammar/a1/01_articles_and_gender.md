# A1 Lesson 1 — Articles and grammatical gender

**Goal:** Recognize masculine and feminine nouns, and select the correct articles for singular and plural nouns.

## 1. Every French noun has a grammatical gender
Nouns are classified as **masculine** or **feminine**. The gender belongs to the word, and does not necessarily reflect anything about the object.

- **un livre** — a book (masculine)
- **une table** — a table (feminine)
- **un café** — a coffee (masculine)
- **une maison** — a house (feminine)

**Learning habit:** Remember a noun *with its article* rather than as a bare word.

## 2. Indefinite articles: a, an, some

| | Masculine | Feminine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | **un** livre | **une** table |
| Plural | **des** livres | **des** tables |

The plural indefinite article **des** works for both genders.

## 3. Definite articles: the

| | Masculine | Feminine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | **le** livre | **la** table |
| Plural | **les** livres | **les** tables |

Before a vowel sound or mute **h**, singular **le** or **la** usually becomes **l’**:
- **l'ami** — the male friend
- **l'amie** — the female friend
- **l'école** — the school

The elided article **l’** does *not* mean the noun has no gender. For instance, **une école** is feminine.

## 4. Plural nouns
Most nouns form a written plural by adding **-s**, although there are exceptions. This final **-s** is usually silent.

- **le livre → les livres**
- **une maison → des maisons**
- **un journal → des journaux** (an irregular pattern to recognize for now)

## 5. How articles are used
- **J'ai un livre.** — I have a book.
- **Le livre est sur la table.** — The book is on the table.
- **J'aime le café.** — I like coffee.

French often uses definite articles with general categories, as in **J'aime le café**.

## 6. Mistakes to avoid
- Using **un** for feminine nouns: **une maison**, not *un maison*.
- Leaving a singular article before a plural noun: **les livres**, not *le livres*.
- Assuming that **l’** identifies a noun's gender.
- Omitting articles just because English would omit them.
